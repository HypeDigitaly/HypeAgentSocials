## 1. The claim-pack architecture

### 1.1 What a claim pack is, and the one thing it is not

A **claim pack** is a named, versioned bundle of three things, all operating **inside the existing claim-gate substrate** (§6.7, §14.3) unless §1.4 says otherwise:

1. **Check-class additions or tightenings** — new check classes, or denser lexicons/lower match thresholds inside an existing class, layered on top of the eleven (soon twelve — §1.3) classes the base pack already runs.
2. **Fact-class groundings** — the fact classes (§6.3, extended at §4 below) a pack's checks read from.
3. **Extraction requirements per language** — Czech and English are both first-class per D-02; a pack that only ships an English lexicon is not shippable.

**Packs are strictly additive over the base pack, and may only add strictness, never remove it** — the same monotonic rule hard excludes (F-J) and the AI-content class (§3.3) already use, applied one layer up. A pack cannot disable a base-pack non-disableable class, cannot narrow a superlative lexicon, cannot loosen a depiction-policy floor a stricter pack set, and cannot exempt itself from the Prohibited-Outcome Gate (§2), which is not a pack member and therefore has nothing a pack could remove.

**A pack is selected by the playbook, not authored by the theme.** Per the decision already taken, "what a post IS for this kind of business" is a playbook-level fact — a restaurant playbook selects the food & beverage pack because restaurants structurally need it, not because an individual tenant's theme config happens to turn it on. A theme running under a playbook inherits every pack that playbook selects and may only tighten within it (denser exclusion lists, a stricter depiction-policy value, an earlier stale-warn threshold) — never opt out of a pack classwise, and never select a *different* pack than the one its playbook has chosen. **Adding a genuinely new regulated vertical means authoring a new pack (or a new playbook that selects an existing one), and that is engineering-plus-legal work, never a tenant-facing configuration action** — this is the direct fix for Defect 1's root cause: today, any tenant can write any claim into their own ledger and have the claim gate call it VERIFIED, because "which legal regime applies to this claim" was never a modelled question at all. After this amendment it is a modelled, engine-owned question, answered by which packs a playbook selects, not by what a tenant asserts.

### 1.2 The base commercial pack — always on, every tenant, every vertical

**All eleven of today's check classes are universal**, because their legal grounding — principally the Unfair Commercial Practices Directive 2005/29/EC ("UCPD") Art. 6 (misleading actions) and Art. 7 (misleading omissions), plus the general "advertising must be recognisable" duty already carried at check class 10's recognisability sub-class — applies to any commercial communication in the EU regardless of sector. Nothing about them is hospitality-specific, health-specific or B2B-specific; a restaurant, a spiritual community page and a B2B lead-gen agency are all equally capable of an unsupported numeric claim, an invented client name or a manufactured-urgency temporal claim. *(UCPD Art. 6/7 as the general basis for the eleven classes: Medium-High confidence — a very well-established provision, not independently re-verified against EUR-Lex primary text this session; see §6, OD-L5.)*

The base pack is therefore: **the eleven existing check classes, unmodified in scope**, plus two amendments this document makes at base-pack level because both close gaps that are vertical-agnostic:

- **The class 1/class 2 relationship is clarified, not changed in strictness** (§5 below carries the full reasoning): a currency/price number that is grounded by a site- or feed-verified F-G/F-P/F-T value at the confidence class 2 already requires does **not** additionally need a class 1 ledger entry for the same number. This removes a redundancy, not a control.
- **A twelfth check class is added: depicted-attribute / real-product imagery** (§3 below carries the full design). It runs at base-pack level, for every tenant, because any tenant — not only hospitality or product/e-commerce — can generate imagery of a real, physical or digital, sellable item (a B2B tenant's product-dashboard screenshot render, a creator/UGC agency's client-product shot). The **engine default is the safe end of its policy range** (Policy A, no depiction of real sellable products — §3.3), and only a playbook whose whole purpose requires product depiction may select a looser value.

**Six check classes are non-disableable at base-pack level**, one more than today's five, because Defect 2 is exactly the shape of gap that made the original five list necessary in the first place — a control a tenant can switch off is not a control: **numeric quantity, currency/price, named entities, endorsement/social proof, required-statement, and (new) depicted-attribute**.

**The Prohibited-Outcome Gate (§2) is not a base-pack member.** It is engine floor beneath the whole pack system, running for every tenant regardless of which packs are selected, and it has no "on/off" state for a pack to inherit or a theme to tighten — describing it as part of the base pack would wrongly imply a pack *could*, in principle, not select it.

## 2. The Prohibited-Outcome Gate — closing Defect 1

### 2.1 Why this cannot be a claim *check* in the substantiation sense

Every one of today's eleven check classes (and the new twelfth) asks a **substantiation question**: is there a ledger entry, a proof record, a resolved fact that this specific claim maps to? That question is the right test for claims that are lawful-if-true-and-evidenced. It is the **wrong test** for a category of claim that EU and Czech law make unlawful **regardless of truth, regardless of evidence, and in some cases regardless of authorisation state a small operator could realistically hold**:

- **Reg. 1924/2006 Art. 10** operates a closed positive-list system for health claims on food: a claim must be authorised and included in the Union register before it may be made, and — critically — **a true, well-evidenced claim that is not on the list is still unlawful**. A ledger entry with a genuine evidence pointer proves the claim is *true*; it does not prove the claim is *authorised*, and the architecture has no fact class today that could even represent "authorised" as distinct from "evidenced." *(Medium confidence on the Art. 10 number specifically; the closed-list mechanism itself is corroborated at Medium-High confidence by a directly-fetched EU Commission page this session — see §0. Open counsel item OD-L1.)*
- **Reg. 1169/2011 Art. 7(3)** goes further for the narrowest case: food information "shall not attribute to any food the property of preventing, treating or curing a human disease, nor refer to such properties" — **full stop, no authorisation pathway exists at all**. There is no ledger entry, however well-sourced, that could ever make "our matcha cures anxiety" lawful. *(Medium-High confidence; OD-L4.)*
- **Dir. 2001/83/EC**, read with its own definition of "medicinal product" as including a product **"presented as"** having properties to treat, prevent or diagnose disease (the "medicinal product by presentation" doctrine, generally understood to sit at Art. 1(2) of the Directive), means a non-pharmaceutical tenant making a therapeutic-outcome claim risks a consequence more severe than an advertising breach: **the product or service itself can be treated as an unauthorised medicinal product**, an entirely separate and more serious regulatory track than an ad-content violation. Title VIII of the same Directive (Arts. 86–100, the advertising provisions, commonly cited via Art. 87) separately requires that advertising not be misleading and not exaggerate a product's properties. *(Medium confidence on both the doctrine and the article numbers; direct EUR-Lex retrieval was attempted this session and blocked — OD-L2.)*
- **MDR (EU) 2017/745 Art. 7**, "Claims," prohibits attributing to a device functions or properties it does not have, or presenting an unclassified product/app as capable of diagnosis or treatment — relevant wherever an offer uses diagnostic- or measurement-flavoured language ("tracks," "measures," "detects") that could imply undeclared medical-device status. *(Medium-High confidence on the article number; OD-L3.)*
- **UCPD Annex I** carries a per-se "blacklist" of practices considered unfair in all circumstances, independent of the general misleading-action test — including, on a widely-cited item (commonly numbered 17), **falsely claiming that a product is able to cure illnesses, dysfunction or malformations**. This is the general backstop that reaches any product category, not only food or medical devices, for the *false*-claim case specifically. *(Medium confidence on the item number; OD-L5.)*

Running "our sessions relieve anxiety" (the launching brief's own example) through the base pack's eleven classes shows exactly why substantiation-shaped checking cannot catch this: if the tenant has a real, evidenced ledger entry — a genuine testimonial, honestly obtained, honestly worded, with permission — the claim passes class 4 (outcome/result, ledger-backed), class 9 (endorsement, permissioned), and every spin criterion S-1 through S-7, because the *pairing* is honest and the *evidence* is real. **The defect is not that the system fails to check; it is that the system checks the wrong thing.** A claim can be perfectly substantiated and still be an unlawful, unauthorised therapeutic-outcome assertion under the instruments above.

### 2.2 What the Gate contains

A deterministic-first, semantic-second pattern (reusing, not reinventing, the architecture §6.7 already specifies for the eleven classes): a dictionary of **condition/dysfunction nouns** (disease names, recognised disorders, and — Czech-specific — colloquial/folk terms for the same) cross-referenced against a dictionary of **outcome verbs and verb-equivalents** implying treatment, cure, prevention, relief, healing, or diagnosis, attributed to a tenant's own offer, service, product, session, or practice. The semantic pass catches paraphrase the dictionary pass cannot ("your worries melt away" implies anxiety relief with zero lexical overlap with either dictionary). The base engine list is deliberately narrow and defensible (recognised diseases/disorders/dysfunctions only); the health & wellness pack (§1.3) densifies it for tenants where the risk is structurally higher.

**The Gate is not one of the twelve numbered check classes**, and does not use the eleven/twelve classes' verdict vocabulary (VERIFIED / SAFE-NON-CLAIM / UNSUPPORTED / CONTRADICTED / DISCLOSURE-MISSING). It has exactly two states: **clear** and **prohibited-pattern-matched**. There is no "verified" state, because — per §2.1 — nothing in this system can make the pattern lawful; the ledger lookup that would normally follow a class match simply never runs.

### 2.3 Where it sits in the gate order

The Gate runs at **every point the claim gate itself runs**, because a rewrite anywhere downstream can reintroduce the pattern (the same principle §6.7/§6.10 already states for the claim gate's own double pass — *any gate that runs before a rewriting step must be re-run after it*):

    generate
      -> SPIN GATE
      -> PROHIBITED-OUTCOME GATE (pass 1, fail fast)   <-- new, runs
      -> CLAIM GATE pass 1                                  before any
      -> VOICE GATE                                         ledger lookup
      -> PROHIBITED-OUTCOME GATE (pass 2)               <-- re-checked,
      -> CLAIM GATE pass 2, final, immutable                voice gate can
      -> PLATFORM GATE                                      reintroduce it
      -> (media only) COST GATE -> media generation -> assembly
      -> POST-ASSEMBLY OVERLAY PASS                     <-- Gate runs here
         (on-screen text and spoken lines, same             too, same reason
          reasoning as the existing overlay claim pass)      §4.4 already
      -> asset QA rubric -> packaging                        gives the overlay
      -> HUMAN REVIEW GATE -> PUBLISH GATE                   claim pass

**The critical property is the ordering relative to the ledger, not relative to the other gates.** Within each pass, the Gate is checked **before class 4 (outcome/result) ever performs its own ledger lookup** — a matched pattern short-circuits the pipeline before the substantiation question is even asked, so there is no code path by which a ledger entry could be consulted for this pattern at all. This is the literal, architectural answer to "a tenant ledger entry... must be unable to authorise it": authorisation is never queried, because the Gate does not query anything that could say yes.

### 2.4 Why it resists the three failure modes it exists to prevent

- **A tenant ledger entry cannot authorise it**, because the Gate's match short-circuits before the ledger lookup (§2.3) — there is no lookup for a ledger entry to satisfy. This is the same "the site can subtract but never add" asymmetry §6.4 already applies to F-H, generalised one step further: here, nothing can add, because nothing is ever asked to.
- **A playbook selection cannot authorise it**, because the Gate is not a pack member (§1.2/§1.3) — it sits beneath the playbook layer, at engine floor, exactly where hard excludes (F-J) and the AI-content class already sit. A playbook may **extend** the Gate's densified pattern list (the health & wellness pack does exactly this), but the playbook API surface has no verb for narrowing or removing entries from the base list, in the same sense §3.3 states the AI-content class "may only tighten, never relax."
- **An operator override cannot authorise it**, because §6.4 already establishes that human run overrides are bounded, not supreme, and may not create commercial facts. This document extends that same enumerated list explicitly: an override may narrow a run's scope but **may not create a therapeutic, medicinal, curative, preventive or diagnostic outcome assertion**, in unattended or interactive mode alike. This is also the one place this amendment departs, deliberately, from the confidence-band degrade trigger's own mode-sensitivity: §6.5's degrade trigger is interactive-relaxable (an operator may knowingly accept MINIMAL from an offline snapshot); the Prohibited-Outcome Gate is **not mode-sensitive at all** — it applies identically in test, interactive and unattended modes, because the harm it prevents is not a data-freshness risk an informed human can knowingly accept, it is a strict-liability-adjacent legal exposure no human approval can cure. The **human review gate** (§11) can approve or reject anything the pipeline *produced*; it has nothing to approve here, because a Gate-blocked assertion never reaches packaging in a form that contains it.

### 2.5 Enforcement and fail-closed behaviour

A match blocks the offending span, exactly like a claim-gate block. From there the ladder **deliberately diverges from the eleven-class ladder**, because downgrade-repair (emit the claim-free variant, strip proof, soften the CTA) is the wrong repair here: the defect is not missing evidentiary support, it is the assertion's *shape*. So:

1. **Bounded regenerate is permitted, constrained to a structural instruction**: remove the disease/condition-outcome framing entirely, fed as a positive constraint naming what may be said instead (describe the practice, not its effect on a named condition) — same mechanics, same per-pack retry budget as the existing ladder (§6.7).
2. **There is no downgrade-repair step that ships a softened version of the same assertion.** On exhaustion of the regenerate allowance, the asset is **dropped**, not shipped in a claim-free-but-still-implying form, with the reason recorded and the rejected draft attached — the same "never silently ship, never silently discard without a note" discipline §6.7 already states.
3. **A repeated trip on the same topic/offer pairing is a named, escalating digest event**, distinct from an ordinary regeneration count: it tells the operator something about the *offer's positioning*, not about prompt quality, and is surfaced as such ("this offer's messaging keeps needing therapeutic-outcome framing removed") rather than folded into the ordinary claim-retry-budget exhaustion message.
4. **Fails closed on its own unavailability, the same shape as the fifth fail-closed trigger (§11.3)**: the deterministic dictionary pass is a local pattern match and should almost never itself be unavailable; the semantic paraphrase pass is a model call and can fail — on that failure, the Gate degrades to deterministic-only verdicts and treats anything ambiguous as a match, never as clear. An asset the Gate cannot fully evaluate does not ship un-evaluated.
5. **Logged per asset**, the same regulatory-defensibility discipline §6.7's closing line already establishes for the claim-check log generally.

### 2.6 The hard edge case, answered honestly

A genuinely licensed clinic, or a lawfully authorised health claim, is a real case the five archetypes will eventually meet — the expressive/spiritual community archetype in particular sits closest to it. The honest v1 answer is that **this system cannot safely serve that tenant with generated, claim-shaped therapeutic-outcome content at all**, for reasons that are not merely a missing config knob:

- There is no fact class today that could represent "this tenant holds a current, scope-limited professional licence entitling them to make regulated therapeutic claims about condition X" — building one requires deciding what counts as evidence of licensure (a national medical or psychotherapy register entry? a specific professional chamber?), who verifies it, and how it is rechecked — a genuine product-plus-legal design task, not a data-entry field.
- Even a verified licence does not uniformly unlock the Gate, because licensure interacts differently with each of the five instruments in §2.1: a licensed Czech psychotherapist may be entitled, under their own professional advertising rules (an unresearched body of law this document does not cover — see OD-L7), to describe their *service* in clinical language; that entitlement does nothing to authorise a *food or wellness product* claim under Reg. 1924/2006, which has its own, separate, EU-level authorisation procedure that licensure at a national professional level does not satisfy.
- Designing a licence-verification and per-instrument authorisation-state model without qualified regulatory-affairs counsel input would itself be exactly the kind of unlawful-claim-laundering risk Defect 1 already demonstrates — the system inventing its own compliance theatre in place of a genuine legal answer.

**The v1 answer is therefore explicit and deliberately unautomated: a genuinely licensed tenant's regulated-claim content is authored and reviewed entirely outside the claim-ledger/claim-gate substrate**, by the operator, by hand — the same posture the architecture already takes toward things it cannot safely automate (rung-3 local staging, the manual AI-label acknowledgement). Whether a "licensed-claims pack" should exist in v2, and what it would need per instrument, is carried forward as OD-L8 rather than designed now.

## 3. Depicted-attribute claim checking — closing Defect 2

### 3.1 When generated imagery constitutes a claim about a real product

An asset **depicts a real sellable item** when (a) its topic/offer pairing (from spin) references an F-B, F-P or F-T catalogue entry that is a physical or photographable item — a prepared dish, a packaged product, a piece of merchandise — rather than an abstract service or software feature, **and** (b) the generated image's subject is presented as, or reads to a viewer as, a depiction of *that specific item* — a product shot, a "here's what's inside the box" image, a plated-dish photo — rather than a category-level mood or lifestyle visual with no specific-item claim attached. A generic "bowl of pasta" mood shot illustrating an article about Italian cuisine is not a depicted-attribute claim; a photorealistic image captioned as tonight's actual special is.

**Legal grounding.** UCPD Art. 6(1)(b) treats "the main characteristics of the product... composition... fitness for purpose... quantity... geographical or commercial origin, or the results to be expected from its use" as a misleading-action ground — an image is exactly a claim vehicle for composition, quantity and appearance. Reg. 1169/2011 Art. 7(1) is more directly on point for food specifically: food information must not be misleading as to a food's nature, identity, properties, composition or quantity, **"in particular... by means of pictures or other similar means"** — the imagery limb is textual, not inferred. *(UCPD Art. 6(1)(b): Medium-High confidence, well-established provision, not independently re-verified this session. FIC Art. 7(1)'s pictures limb: Medium-High confidence, same caveat. See OD-L5, OD-L4.)*

**The burned-in AI-generated disclosure does not cure this**, and the reasoning is worth stating precisely because it is easy to conflate: the Art. 50 AI Act disclosure answers a *process* question — is this synthetic — while UCPD/FIC misleading-imagery liability answers a *substance* question — does this depiction correspond to reality. Disclosing that a photo is AI-generated no more excuses a materially false depiction of the actual dish than disclosing "this photo has been retouched" would excuse one under ordinary advertising practice. The two obligations are cumulative, exactly as the architecture plan already establishes for AI-labelling generally (§14.6: "cumulative with, not a substitute for").

### 3.2 The reference-grounding requirement

**An image depicting a real sellable item must derive from tenant-supplied genuine reference material.** Mechanically, this reuses generation modes the architecture already has rather than inventing a new one: §4.3's existing **reference-to-video / reference-to-image mode**, already specified as "holds a product consistent across clips," is the safe generation mode for real-item depiction; plain generative text-to-image ("imagine a dish like ours") is the unsafe mode and is the one this control exists to close off.

The reference asset itself gets a **new fact class, F-W — reference imagery for real sellable items** (§4), and its identity is recorded on the **existing per-asset provenance record** (§14.6/§8.13) as an additional field alongside routed-model identity, generation timestamp and licence snapshot — a new field on an existing record, not a new subsystem. The provenance record additionally carries a **tenant attestation** that the reference is a current, accurate depiction of the item (not a stock photo, not a supplier's marketing photo of a different portion size, not a discontinued item's old photo) — the same staleness discipline §6.5/§6.6 already apply to every other fact class applies here: an unrefreshed reference beyond its freshness window degrades that item's depiction eligibility, not the whole pack.

### 3.3 The three-value depiction policy

| Value | Meaning | When selected |
|---|---|---|
| **Policy A — no depiction** | Real sellable products may never be depicted in generated imagery for this tenant; product/offer discussion stays textual, imagery stays category-level or brand-mood only | **Engine default and floor.** Applies to every tenant unless a playbook whose whole purpose requires product depiction selects otherwise |
| **Policy B — reference-grounded** | Generated imagery may depict a real sellable item, built from a linked, current F-W reference asset per §3.2, with the review-package attestation of §3.4 | Selected at playbook level only by **food & beverage** and **product/e-commerce** — the two playbooks whose entire reason for existing includes depicting what is actually served or sold |
| **Policy C — decorative/illustrative only** | Generated imagery may depict a product *category* or scene type, not claimed to be the specific tenant offer, structurally framed (via caption/CTA template) as illustrative rather than documentary | Available **within** the food & beverage and product/e-commerce playbooks for mood content that makes no specific-item claim (an ambience reel is Policy C even inside a Policy B playbook); not a substitute for Policy B wherever a specific item is actually being promoted — "tonight's special" is a specific-item claim and requires B, not C |

A theme operating under a Policy-B playbook **may tighten to Policy A** (a tenant with poor reference photography may prefer no product imagery at all) but **may never loosen a playbook-set B down to ungrounded generative imagery of a specific item** — the same tightening-only relationship §1.1 states for packs generally.

### 3.4 Human attestation and what the review pack must show

**A new, separately-recorded acknowledgement**, alongside the existing AI-label acknowledgement (§14.6) and editorial-responsibility fields (§3.3/§11.4), for exactly the reason those are already separate rather than folded into general pack approval — bundling is how a busy operator skips it: *"I have compared this generated image against the linked reference asset(s) and confirm the depiction is not materially misleading as to composition/contents, portion size or quantity, packaging, colour, and any accompaniment not included in the actual offer."*

For that attestation to be a judgement rather than a rubber stamp, **the review pack must show, not merely link to**:

- **The reference asset and the generated image side by side, in the review package itself** — the same "both conflicting values side by side with sources and timestamps" pattern §6.4/§6.5 already establish for red-flag conflicts, reused here for the identical reason: a reviewer who has to go fetch the comparison will not.
- **The reference asset's own provenance and freshness state** — when supplied, by whom, and whether it sits inside or outside its freshness window — using the same staleness machinery already built for every other fact class, not a new date-tracking mechanism.
- **Which specific claim dimensions the reference actually grounds** — a reference photo may ground packaging (the box is shown) without grounding contents (the box was never opened in the photo); the reviewer needs the tool to say so explicitly rather than assume total coverage from a single reference image.
- **The generation mode actually used** — reference-grounded versus plain generative — surfaced the same way a requested-versus-delivered route mismatch is already surfaced (R-08, §5.6), so a silent fallback to ungrounded generation (a model substitution, a route degrade) is visible before approval, not only discoverable in the ledger afterwards.

**Fail-closed precondition.** An asset flagged as depicting a real sellable item, under Policy B, with no linked reference asset in its provenance record, **cannot reach the review package as a publish candidate at all** — a new precondition alongside the existing AI-label-required flag and provenance-record-completeness precondition already gating publish-readiness (§14.6/§7.7), degrading the same way an asset with a damaged provenance record already does (the existing Phase 3 acceptance criterion): to plan-only, never silently shipped.

### 3.5 Designed for product promotion to work

The operator has confirmed real product promotion is on the critical path, and this control is sized accordingly: at Policy B, with adequate reference photography already on hand, the marginal reviewer cost is a glance at two images side by side — the same order of effort as the legibility and disclosure checks already in §3.5's review-depth table, not a new bottleneck. The expensive failure mode this closes is specific and real (a restaurant depicting a dish it does not serve, a seller depicting packaging or contents that do not exist), and the fix is grounding, not prohibition.

### 3.6 Check class 12, and its two enforcement points

Depicted-attribute checking is fully **substantiation-shaped** — unlike the Prohibited-Outcome Gate, it is cured by evidence (a genuine reference asset), so it belongs inside the ordinary claim-gate substrate as **check class 12**, reusing the existing verdict vocabulary (a grounded depiction is VERIFIED against its F-W reference; an ungrounded one under Policy B is UNSUPPORTED and blocks).

It runs at **two points**, mirroring two enforcement patterns the architecture already uses rather than inventing a third:

1. **At keyframe acceptance (§4.2a)**, as a **sixth rubric item**, binary and non-negotiable like items 2 (no legible text) and 4 (person-policy compliance) — because reference-grounding compliance is a compliance surface, not a quality one, and belongs at the same cheap, pre-clip-spend approval point §4.1 already identifies as the single most important economic control in the system.
2. **As check class 12 within claim gate pass 2 and the post-assembly overlay pass (§4.4)**, verifying the final asset's depicted subject against its provenance record's reference link — catching the case where an accepted, grounded keyframe was later swapped for an ungrounded replacement during a repair or regenerate cycle, the same "any gate that runs before a rewriting step must be re-run after it" principle §6.7/§6.10 already state, applied to imagery instead of text.

### 4.2 The audit's real finding: which existing blocking classes must become legitimately-emptiable

Four of today's blocking classes are marked "may not be empty" (§6.3's table) for a reason that is really about **offers**, not about brand identity or excludes generally: **F-B (offer catalogue), F-C (capability statements), F-E (CTA set), and F-F (pricing policy)** are all defined *in terms of* the offer catalogue — F-C describes what each offer does, F-E is a cross-product over offers, F-F is a rule about whether offer prices may be stated. A tenant with genuinely zero commercial offers at a given time — the expressive/spiritual community archetype in the general case, a seasonal restaurant between openings, any tenant deliberately pausing sales — cannot resolve any of the four as currently specified, and because all four gate at "Step 1" of the band computation (§6.5), the whole pipeline closes rather than degrading gracefully.

| Class | Recommendation | Safety consequence of the change |
|---|---|---|
| **F-B** | Legitimately emptiable at the whole-catalogue level | **None negative.** This is the same "resolved-empty is a first-class, safe, generative state" pattern §6.3 already applies to F-H and F-I: the generator is told it has zero approved offers and writes teaching/community content with no product-path or commercial-incentive CTA available. Every offer-dependent CTA precondition already requires the offer to resolve (§6.9), so consumers were already built defensively — the fix is purely at the confidence-band gate, not at the consumers |
| **F-C** | Legitimately emptiable, **but only jointly with F-B being empty** — not an independent emptiness rule | **None, and this narrower framing is deliberate.** If F-B has offers, F-C for those offers stays non-emptyable exactly as today, because the negative-capability discipline against autonomy inflation (§6.3's own stated reason for F-C) is too important to relax for any tenant with something to overclaim about. Making F-C independently emptiable would let a tenant have live offers with no negative-capability grounding, silently losing check class 6's negative-list basis — the joint rule closes that gap explicitly |
| **F-E** | Legitimately emptiable at the whole-set level, following F-B | **None new.** An empty CTA set defaults every asset to the "none" outcome §6.9 already names. The content-CTA class remains available regardless (it is not offer-scoped), which is worth stating explicitly so implementers do not accidentally couple its availability to F-E's non-emptiness by habit |
| **F-F** | **Not made emptiable in the same way.** F-F is a *rule*, not offer data, and the correct fix is a safe engine-level default — "prices are never stated" — that applies automatically whenever F-G/F-P/F-T price *values* are themselves empty or unresolved, rather than requiring a tenant with nothing to price to author a pricing rule by hand | **Deliberately not the same fix as the other three.** Making F-F emptiable in the same sense as F-B/F-C/F-E would create a state where the system does not *know* whether prices may be stated — exactly the dangerous "unresolved, not empty" state §6.3 already warns against for F-H/F-J. F-F stays a resolved-value class; the engine supplies the safe default so a no-product tenant never has to touch it |

**F-T inherits F-B's fix exactly**, being structurally F-B's e-commerce specialisation (§4.1): a product/e-commerce tenant with a genuinely empty catalogue (a pre-launch tenant, a seasonal closure) resolves the same way F-B does — legitimately empty at catalogue level, unresolved and blocking per item once named.

## 5. Price handling inversion

### 5.1 The latent inconsistency, named precisely

Check class 1 (numeric quantity) states: "every number in a claim position must map to a ledger entry... the default is claim — fail closed." Check class 2 (currency/price) states: a price is "allowed only if the exact value and terms exist **at full confidence** and the pricing policy permits stating them" — and §6.5's band table ties "full confidence" to F-G, which is **site-verified**, not ledger-authored. As written, a price number is simultaneously required to have a **tenant-authored ledger entry** (class 1's literal text) and a **site-verified F-G value at FULL band** (class 2's literal text), with nothing in §6.7 stating whether both are required or whether class 2 alone suffices. For a B2B tenant with one or two prices that rarely change, requiring both is tolerable duplicate entry. For a restaurant with twenty to forty menu items changing weekly, or a product catalogue with dozens of SKUs, requiring both is exactly the "hand-enter the whole menu into a claim ledger weekly" burden this document is asked to avoid — and it is a burden the underlying legal risk does not actually demand, because a live, fetched price is at least as reliably checkable as a human-maintained ledger row that can go stale exactly as easily.

**Resolution: class 2 is self-sufficient for price-shaped numeric claims and does not additionally require a class 1 ledger entry.** Class 1's rule is read, at base-pack level, as: every number in a claim position maps to a ledger entry **or** to a class-2-qualifying, site-/feed-verified F-G/F-P/F-T value. This removes a redundancy the two classes' literal text left ambiguous; it does not weaken either class's own grounding rule, both of which stand unchanged.

### 5.2 How price-bearing verticals work operationally

The existing precedence machinery is already the right one for this case — §6.4 built F-G's "site is binding, never tie-broken" rule for exactly the reason that matters here ("the live site is the thing a prospect can actually read an hour before seeing our post"). What is missing is not a new mechanism but wiring the existing one to a higher item count:

1. **The tenant's own live menu/catalogue page, or a structured feed where one exists (a POS export, an e-commerce platform's own product feed), is the F-P/F-T source of record**, verified through the **same declared verification-page-set mechanism §6.6 already built** for capability-statement and claim-ledger checking — the per-run verification budget is already explicitly scoped to cover "liveness, price values, trial terms and offer status" (§6.6). No new fetch mechanism is needed; the existing one is exercised at a materially higher item count, and the **fetch budget/timeout knob (§10.3) is sized per playbook** to accommodate that count.
2. **A structured feed is recommended over free-text page scraping wherever the tenant has one**, because parsing a feed is far more reliable at forty-item scale than HTML scraping of a menu page — a different source *type* for the same declared-verification role, not a new architectural idea.
3. **The claim ledger (F-H) is not the price source for these verticals and is not required to carry per-item price rows** — closing the "whole menu into the ledger weekly" concern directly. Menu and catalogue items live in F-P/F-T, never duplicated into F-H.
4. **Confidence-band computation extends the existing per-entry quarantine mechanic** (§6.4: "Contradiction quarantines that entry," already true for F-H) **to F-P/F-T at per-item granularity.** One stale or unreachable menu item does not fail the whole pack — it individually degrades that item's content to price-free, non-numeric framing, exactly as one bad ledger entry is individually quarantined today. This is the piece of machinery that makes forty independent fetch targets operationally survivable: an occasional miss is expected at that scale, and the system must not let one 404 shut down the day's run.
5. **The never-tie-break rule stands unmodified for these verticals.** A menu-page-versus-feed disagreement on a specific item's price is still a red-flag stop for that item, for the identical reason §6.4 already gives — a wrong price is a commercial promise and a Czech consumer-protection matter (C7 §2.9, itself flagged Low-Medium confidence pending OD-25). The inversion changes *who authors the number* (site/feed, not a typed ledger row) and *the granularity of failure* (per item, not per pack) — never the tie-break discipline itself.

**What this document explicitly does not design: price-reduction claims.** F-G already names "discount and affiliate terms" among its contents, and the product/e-commerce archetype will need to say "was €40, now €25" — a materially different check from stating a plain current price, because the Omnibus Directive's prior-price rule (§6, OD-L6) requires the *comparison* itself to be lawful, not just the current figure. That check class is a genuine scope gap this document leaves open rather than quietly assumes away — see OD-L9.

## Summary of key decisions

- **Base commercial pack** = today's eleven check classes, universal, plus a clarified class 1/class 2 relationship and a new, universal, non-disableable **check class 12 (depicted-attribute)** defaulting to the safe end of a three-value depiction policy.
- **Five packs** (food & beverage, health & wellness, financial/monetary, product/e-commerce, plus the base pack every archetype shares) are additive over the base, selected by playbook, tightening-only. Financial/monetary is specified now as a registry entry with no current archetype selecting it.
- **The Prohibited-Outcome Gate** is new, engine-level, and deliberately **not** a claim check, not a pack member, and not reachable by ledger entry, playbook selection, or operator override — it short-circuits before any ledger lookup, sits beneath the playbook layer, and applies identically in every mode. It fails closed, drops rather than downgrade-repairs, and the honest v1 answer for a genuinely licensed actor is that they must work entirely outside this pipeline.
- **Check class 12 (depicted-attribute)** closes Defect 2 via a reference-grounding requirement reusing the existing reference-to-image generation mode and provenance record, a three-value depiction policy defaulting safe, and a new, separately-recorded human attestation backed by a side-by-side review-pack comparison — designed so product promotion works, not so it is banned.
- **Eight new fact classes** (F-O through F-W) give the missing verticals a home; **F-B, F-C and F-E are recommended legitimately-emptiable** (with F-C's emptiness deliberately tied to F-B's), closing the no-product-tenant-unrunnable defect the audit found; **F-F is fixed by a safe engine default, not by emptiness**, for a reason distinct from the other three.
- **Price handling** is inverted for price-bearing verticals by naming and closing a real redundancy between check classes 1 and 2, and by extending the existing per-entry quarantine mechanic (already used for F-H) to F-P/F-T at per-item granularity — no new fetch mechanism, no ledger-entry-per-menu-item burden, and the never-tie-break rule stands unmodified.
- **Eleven open counsel items (OD-L1–OD-L11)** are logged, each with a recommended interim posture that never relaxes a control pending confirmation; price-reduction claims (OD-L9) and the Financial/monetary pack's substantive grounding (OD-L11) are named as genuine, unresearched scope gaps this document leaves open rather than quietly assumes away.
