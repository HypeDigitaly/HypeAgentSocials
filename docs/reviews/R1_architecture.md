## 0. Headline

The plan is substantially better than the median Stage-4 artifact. Its source-access honesty (§2), brand-truth layer (§6), money-safety design (§5.6, §8.5, §8.13) and self-review method (§18) are genuinely strong, and it repeatedly re-derives consequences of the operator's overrules rather than carrying superseded text. That quality is what makes the remaining defects worth naming precisely: they are not sloppiness, they are three places where the plan asserts a control it does not actually have, plus a set of internal contradictions that would each cost an implementer a wrong decision.

**Counts: 3 blockers · 13 majors · 7 minors.**

The three blockers share one shape — *the plan claims coverage it cannot demonstrate*: LLM spend is claimed to be capped and is not; the risk-log coverage map claims completeness and is missing four rows; and the flagship worked example demonstrates the opposite of the plan's own safe-by-default rule.

## 4. Verification of the assembler's four self-flagged weak spots

**(a) §10 defaults — safe-by-default and consistent with the modes table?** Mostly yes, and the safety-critical ones are right: mode defaults to test, both cadence knobs default off, publish allowlist empty by construction, unattended draft-creation off, hero auto-promote off, tier ceiling standard, all eleven check classes on with five non-disableable, event and commercial-incentive CTA classes off until preconditions resolve, AI-content class defaults to the strictest value. **Two failures.** The dry-run default (on in test) is contradicted by the plan's own worked example (B3). And the knob surface is mis-scoped and larger than the plan believes (M8). Is ~127 knobs itself a defect? Partly — but the sharper problem is not the count, it is that the count is understated by about 30% in the very risk row (W3-01) whose mitigation is sized against it, and that at least four machine-scoped knobs and two engine-scoped knobs are filed in the per-theme block.

**(b) §15.3's completeness claim — falsify it.** Falsified. See B2. Four of forty-three risk-log rows are absent from the coverage table, and one of them is the assembler's own W3-01.

**(c) Appendix A must not hard-code volatile prices as normative.** **Passes.** Appendix A's header states plainly that costs are read from the model registry's 2026-08-06 snapshot and that the operator's real forecast is computed at run time with its snapshot date displayed; §5.4 and §12.1 repeat the mechanism; §0.3 row 9 carries the prices with a recheck-by date. The arithmetic is also internally sound (A.6 sums to $1.91 against the stated $2.10 forecast; A.9's $0.30 regenerate yields $2.21). One residual issue is a sizing consequence rather than a normativity failure — see m4.

**(d) OD-8 half-open while §17 Phase 4 leans on it — roadmap-coherent?** **Sequencing is coherent** (OD-8 decides at Stage 5; Phase 4 consumes it later). **The acceptance criterion is not load-bearing**, which is the real problem — see M11.

## 7. Verdict

**Ready for Stage 5 after the three blockers and the thirteen majors are fixed — not as-is, and not needing rework.**

This is a repair job, not a redesign. Nothing in §1–§18 is structurally wrong: the pipeline shape, the connector taxonomy, the per-fact-class precedence model, the keyframe-first economics, the write-ahead spend ledger and the one-choke-point publish gate are all sound, and several of them are better than what the research briefs handed the assembler. The plan also does the hard honest things — it concedes the assignment's List A is obsolete, it argues browser automation *out* rather than in, it states that its publishing bridge is unverified in five separate places, and it writes its own adversarial review method in §18.

What it does not yet do is hold to its own standard in three specific places where it asserts a control it lacks. The LLM wallet is uncapped while the document says it is capped (B1). The risk coverage map claims exhaustiveness and drops four rows including the assembler's own highest-likelihood risk (B2). And the worked example the plan tells its reviewer to read first spends money in the mode the plan calls safe-by-default (B3). Each is a paragraph to fix and each would be expensive to discover in Phase 4, which is exactly the trade §18.1 describes.

Of the majors, four are contradictions between two sections that each read as authoritative (M1 money-safety identity, M4 offline-snapshot degrade, M8 knob scoping, M13 what the resolver actually governs); five are missing enforcement behind a stated commitment (M2 pack-level voice cost, M3 gate unavailability, M5 keyframe auto-acceptance, M9 Czech framing, M11 Phase-4 discrimination); and four are legibility or completeness defects that will cost the operator time at Stage 5 or the implementer time later (M6 unnamed decision options, M7 the absent prompt/skill layer, M10 the cs formula in the worked example, M12 the unmarked void in the evidence base).

On the mandated review-risk areas: RA-1, RA-2, RA-4, RA-5 and RA-8's publish half are satisfied and in places exemplary; RA-3 and RA-7 are satisfied with named defects; **RA-6 is not satisfied** and is the one area where the plan's claim and its content genuinely diverge. On Stage 4 coverage, all eighteen items are present, thirteen genuinely and five thinly. On the ten success criteria, eight are genuine and two are thin.

Recommended disposition for the Stage-5 aggregator: **approve with changes**, with §15.3, §11.1/§8.11 (budget), Appendix A's mode header, §8.5/§8.7, §6.5/R-15, §10.4/§10.5 and §16's option columns named as the required revisions, and M7's prompt/skill subsection added as new material.
