# R3 Marketing Review — HypeAgentSocials Architecture Plan
## Wave 4 Adversarial Assessment

**Reviewer:** R3 (Senior Content Marketer)  
**Review Date:** 2026-08-06  
**Scope:** Pipeline → publishable | Voice bar per language | Platform adaptation | Operator walkthrough usability | North-star fit | Appendix A worked example  
**Mandate:** Will this system actually produce content a real Czech AI/lead-gen company can publish and win with? Find where the machine produces technically-compliant but commercially dead output.

## FINDINGS

**blocker | §2.3, §2.7 | The day-1 source portfolio has NO primary carrier of Czech B2B virality signal and the ranking formula handles Czech through numeric omission — safe but silent. | The Czech composite drops virality entirely (stated honestly at §2.7), which is correct given the data absence. However, the "under-evidenced ≠ poor fit" framing in §2.7 and §13.2 does NOT materially prevent silent language degradation if operators don't actively police per-language candidate set sizes. Theme-readiness validation at §13.2 nominally asserts "every configured language produces a non-empty candidate set" — a necessary control. But: (a) "non-empty" at minimum volume produces packs that are technically valid but commercially anemic; (b) the readiness check runs at theme-load time, not at every run; (c) no run-time per-language volume floor exists; (d) Czech-language content surviving on demand-modifier + operator-fed curated-inbox + inverted ad-creative signal is materially thinner than English's multi-family corroboration. Czech packs will be real — but whether they're worth publishing monthly is a decision the operator carries alone. The design does not optimize Czech signal capture or weight Czech sources more heavily. **Recommendation:** Add an optional per-language minimum virality/evidence band (set per theme, default loose, alarmable when hit) so a recurring pattern of "Czech pack is thin but technically not empty" surfaces as a named pattern rather than dissolving into weekly operator decision fatigue. OR lock Czech-first curation as a weekly required ritual (not optional), because the current design makes that ritual invisible from an automated-run perspective.**

**major | §3.1, §15.4 (W2-08 replacement) | The six Czech design commitments are architectural but not all are measurable gates, and the "measurable revisit trigger" (commitment 6) is deferred governance without operational teeth. | Commitments 1–5 (recipe, framing, register, understatement, production floor) are mostly enforceable: carousel-to-reel forces the recipe; the Czech judge rubric can encode framing rules (problem statement versus hook); register discipline is checkable (vykání default); understatement is a judge-dimension (empirically higher weight); the production-floor checklist is a hard gate with specific items (TTS prosody, glyph coverage, no English audio). Commitment 6 — "a measurable revisit trigger, logged rather than assumed" — is governance theater unless OD-22 is resolved concretely before Phase 4 production starts. The plan states W2-08a as a "replacement mitigation" to show Czech short-form is not cheap, but the mitigation chain terminates in "review-decision-store rejection codes + operator's own read of engagement quality" (§15.4), which is exactly the unstructured operator judgment the original mitigation tried to avoid. No KPI exists (click-through, engagement rate, lead-to-close velocity, brand-sentiment shift). No baseline. No measurement infrastructure. If Czech short-form underperforms, the operator has no instrumentation to know it for weeks and no decision logic to act on it. The revisit trigger reads as "we'll know it when we see it" masked as rigor. **Recommendation:** Lock OD-22 to a specific trigger NOW (e.g., "after 20 published Czech short-form assets" or "after 6 weeks, whichever comes first, compare week-over-week engagement to English baseline at p<0.05"). Make the trigger a hard config knob (not a manual judgment). Have Phase 4 acceptance include at least 5 Czech short-form test runs so the baseline exists before production.**

**major | §6.9, §12.2 | The mapping-distance rule (direct/adjacent/far) is solo logic with no platform context, and "far-distance with no offer" is publishable on LinkedIn/Facebook but risky on TikTok where format expects utility. | The design correctly states that far-distance topics carry no product CTA and no capability elaboration (§14.1, S-4). For LinkedIn and blog, this is sensible: pure thought leadership on an adjacent pain point builds authority. BUT: on TikTok/Reels, a 15–30 second pure-education-no-pitch format reads differently. The exemplar corpus evidence (from C5, §1 and working posts from Winning Posts file) shows short-form almost never lands as pure value — TikTok/Reels winning posts have a hook, a utility, and a soft offer-pivot ("if this resonates, DM me" or "link in bio"). A far-distance topic on TikTok becomes "here's a random pain you might have, no solution, no CTA, go read our blog," which leaves the viewer suspended and is why short-form conversion is harder than LinkedIn carousels. The spin gate will not catch this: it only checks that forced relevance is absent (S-3 honesty test); it does not check platform-fit of the distance decision itself. **Recommendation:** Add to the per-destination framing rules (which exist at §3.3 but are hard constraints only) an advisory on minimum mapping distance per platform — e.g., TikTok/Reels default to adjacent-or-closer, farther distances require a named soft bridge. Make this visible in Appendix A's platform-gate section so operators internalize the pattern.**

**major | §4.2–4.8, §12.1 | The video cost forecast is accurate but the delivery (always promised, sometimes generated) is not surfaced with sufficient prominence in the digest, risking operator surprise at pack completion. | The cost gate is airtight: pre-submission, four-level caps, budget reads from registry at runtime, forecast shown with snapshot date. §12.1 specifies the digest carries cost "prominent, decomposed per topic." Appendix A traces this correctly: forecast $2.10 vs cap $6.00, ultimately $1.91 spent, with pending media flagged. HOWEVER: the operator cannot easily see in the digest WHY certain assets are plan-only (budget capped mid-run) versus deliberately held (confidence band too low, site-first rule, language coherence). The `completed-with-pending-media` exit code (§4.7, §17 Phase 4) is honest but lumps two unrelated outcomes: (a) job still rendering at wind-down = normal and recoverable; (b) budget cap hit mid-pack = material and usually visible in the digest. Appendix A shows no example of mid-pack cap-hit, so the operator cannot see how that reads. **Recommendation:** Add one synthetic worked example to Appendix A: a run that hits budget mid-topic, showing exactly which assets were generated before the cap and which were marked "generation skipped — budget capped." Make sure the digest template shows a per-topic "this topic was partial due to budget" line so it is not confused with a degrade or a platform block. Ensure Phase 5 acceptance criteria includes at least one real cap-hit test.**

**major | §2.7, §2.8 | The dedupe index references "trajectory x prior-pack state" (§2.8) but trajectory is never defined — operationally unclear how a topic is recognized as "same cluster evolved" vs "new sighting of a zombie topic." | The ranking section correctly notes that collected signals get a "topic cluster key" (canonical key, near-dup fingerprint) and the dedupe index tracks first-seen date and cross-day state (§2.6, §2.8). The mechanism text says "dedupe index consulted (trajectory x prior-pack state) → top-N cap applied AFTER filtering" but defines neither "trajectory" nor how it gates entry into the top-N. Appendix A shows "same" status and notes "a related cluster from three weeks ago is noted as adjacent-but-distinct" but doesn't say HOW that decision was made or what would make a human or an operator review question it. The operator needs to know: does the system auto-suppress topics that appeared in the last week, 14 days, or 30 days? If a topic is hot again (second wave), what triggers reranking it? If the operator rejects a topic one week, will it reappear the next? None of that is specified. **Recommendation:** Add a concrete section 2.8a detailing the dedupe window (recommend 30 days default), the re-entry rule (new signal count + freshness decay override), and a "rejected topic reappearance" rule (e.g., "manual rejection suppresses for 60 days unless corroboration count exceeds the prior run by >2 families"). Make this a visible config knob (OD not outstanding). Ensure Phase 1 acceptance includes a test: feed the same topic twice with same freshness, verify it dedupe-blocks; feed it a third time with a new news cycle, verify it re-enters and states why.**

**major | §14.2, §14.4 | The Czech voice judge rubric is concrete but the "eleven-dimension" claim at §14.4 lists only five concrete dimensions (vykání default, register discipline, calque blocklist, code-switching allowlist, higher human-voice weight) — the other six are implied as inherited from structural/lexicon layers or not specified. | C5 §3 describes a rubric built from corpus evidence; §14.4 references it as "an eleven-dimension judge rubric." But §14.4's own table in the Czech section contains only the five items listed above, plus "code-switching allowlist is a permission list, not a block list." That's stated correctly but is six explicit items at most, not eleven. The confusing part: are the other five Czech-specific dimensions, or are they the same five that the English rubric (specifically: hook shape, specificity/proof anchoring, personal stake, rhythm, structure/formatting, CTA target-vs-tone, absence of banned patterns) also evaluate? If they're inherited, the rubric needs to state that explicitly, because a Czech post's personal-stake dimension or rhythm will likely feel different from an English B2B post (Czech professionals' writing is often more formal, more sentence-complex, more paratactic). The exemplar corpus for Czech does not exist yet as of Phase 0 (acknowledged in §17), so the actual empirical basis for Czech voice scoring is weaker than English's. **Recommendation:** Clarify in §14.4 that the eleven dimensions are [five inherited from English + five Czech-specific + one Czech-override (higher human-voice weight)]. In Phase 0, do not lock the final judge rubric; set it as a calibration experiment: build the Czech golden set with the hypothesized rubric, run judge vs. human on it, and let the golden-set agreement data inform which dimensions actually differentiate Czech voice for this brand. Treat Phase 0's Czech rubric as a hypothesis, not a law.**

**major | §3.3, §6.9 | The platform-gate hard constraints (character counts, aspect ratios, durations, link behavior) are correct but the CTA-language-coherence rule (when a destination page has no local version) produces degraded CTAs by default, which works but makes Czech language-parity look incomplete at publish time. | Appendix A demonstrates this cleanly: Czech product page returns 404, so the Czech LinkedIn asset degrades from product-path CTA to content CTA pointing at an article (A.4). This is handled correctly — the pack states the reason, the operator sees it, it's not silently wrong. BUT: from a Czech user's perspective, clicking a Czech post's "read the article" link takes them to an article in Czech (✓), but a follow-up interest in the product cycles back through English (✗). The design does not mandate that themes resolve this at theme-readiness time; it defers to operator discretion. The consequence: if the Czech product domain is not yet translated, Czech short-form can run (because content CTA works), but it's building funnel friction by design. The "language coherence" rule is structural safeguard, but it doesn't prevent the operator from publishing with a known downgrade. **Recommendation:** Add a theme-readiness assertion at §13.2: "Every configured language has a product CTA destination URL that resolves and returns content in that language" (or explicitly: "Czech product page 404 is expected and operator has accepted degraded CTA for Czech for the next N weeks"). Make this failure visible at theme validation time so degraded Czech is a *logged decision*, not a silent operational state that compounds weekly. Option: allow a config flag "czech-language-complete = false" that survives the run digest as a banner so the operator doesn't forget.**

**minor | §4.6, §5.4 | The four spend tiers (plan-only, draft, standard, hero) are clear but no guidance on when to promote from draft to standard in practice, risking slow iteration for the operator. | The design correctly gates hero auto-select (never in any mode except explicit promotion by human, §4.6); the cost figures ($0.30 workhorse video, $1.25 quality, etc.) are rooted in A2's verified 2026-08-06 snapshot and regenerate on each run. The economics math (§5.4 cost table) is accurate. HOWEVER: the decision of when a keyframe-draft preview is "good enough to commit standard spend" is left to operator judgment. §3.5 says video review is 20–30 minutes per finished video, which means a single keyframe review (probably 1–2 minutes) is insufficient to make a spend decision confidently. Operators will either (a) approve many draft keyframes and promote them to standard sight-unseen, defeating the keyframe gate, or (b) review draft motion previews in full, adding 10–15 minutes to the decision loop and slowing iteration. A third option: supply a draft-tier → standard-tier decision matrix showing cost difference ($0.05–$0.08 per draft motion vs. $0.30 standard clip) and recommend a sample strategy (e.g., "if 3 of 5 hook candidates pass draft motion smoothly, promote the best one to standard and reject the others"). **Recommendation:** Add a short "operator decision workflow" section in §12 detailing the keyframe-acceptance ritual: how long to spend, what to check, and when to promote vs. reject. Include an illustrative decision matrix (draft cost vs. standard cost vs. time-to-review trade-off) in Phase 3 and Phase 4 acceptance criteria.**

**minor | §7.4, §11.2 | The single publish-enforcement point (§7.4) is architectural and correct, but the read-mode-resolver logic (§11.2) for deciding which destinations are allowed per mode is delegated to code and is not specified in plain language here, risking implementation gaps. | The design correctly centralizes all side-effect decisions at §11.2 ("one resolver, two specialised gates"). The publish allowlist is read per mode; side-effect decisions (media generation spend, Postiz integration) check the same mode resolver. But §11.2 does not show the resolver's output contract — i.e., what does a call to "is publish allowed for destination D in mode M" return? The resolver is introduced but its interface is not specified. This is a small abstraction risk: implementation will need to specify, and if the spec was not locked at review time, a gap could emerge. **Recommendation:** Add a one-paragraph specification of the mode-capability-resolver interface in §11.2: input (destination, action), output (allowed yes/no + reason code), and the precedence rule (e.g., "live-prep mode allows all publish actions for allowlisted destinations; test mode forbids all; staging allows drafts only"). This is small but will save implementation time and will let implementers catch any missed destination case.**

**minor | §12.2, Appendix A | The spin rationale captured per asset (topic id, pain, segment, offer, mapping distance, CTA class, fact-usage trace) is correct and powerful, but is not shown in a human-readable form in Appendix A's example pack, only described in prose. | Appendix A.2–A.4 trace the spin and A.8 lists "the spin rationale" as a per-asset record. ## VERDICT PARAGRAPH

The architecture plan is technically sound and treats Czech as genuinely first-class (not a translation), which passes the north-star mandate. The research-to-rank-to-spin pipeline correctly routes away from forced product placement and has strong gates against invented claims. The voice enforcement is layered (lexicon → structural → judge → regenerate → escalate), and the Czech rubric is concrete where it exists (register discipline, calque blocklist, higher human-voice weight). The operator walkthrough is realistic: copy review 1–2 minutes, video 20–30 minutes, pack scan 2 minutes for 5–6 topics in under 30 minutes total, with granular rejection (per-asset, per-topic, per-pack).

However, the plan has FIVE structural gaps that risk commercially dead output:

1. **Czech language will degrade silently** if operators don't police per-run candidate-set size. The readiness check runs at theme-load, not per-run. A "thin but non-empty" Czech pack is valid by the system, publishable by operator choice, but unsustainable at scale. The design does not optimize Czech signal or warn on recurring thinness.

2. **The Czech short-form safety mitigation (six design commitments) is enforced but not measured**. OD-22's revisit trigger is deferred as governance theater: "we'll know it when we see it." No KPI baseline, no measurement infrastructure, no velocity warning.

3. **Mapping distance has no platform awareness**. Far-distance topics (pure value, no offer) read differently on TikTok (suspended, no follow action) than on LinkedIn (thought leadership, OK). The spin gate doesn't catch platform-fit mismatches.

4. **Three critical asymmetries in brand-truth resolution** (excludes monotonic, site subtracts capability, silence ≠ disagreement) are documented once in prose but not re-emphasized at conflict-outcome time, risking silent misapplication by operators or implementers.

5. **Czech language-coherence degradation is documented but not flagged as a logged operator decision**. When a Czech product page returns 404, the CTA degrades to content. This is correct handling, but if unremarked, it's operationally invisible after the first week.

The system will produce publishable content where sources are strong (English, LinkedIn, developer discourse) and will degrade honestly where sources are thin (Czech, trend platforms, curated inbox). It will not publish false claims or cheap-looking generated video. But it will require an alert operator to prevent Czech from becoming a slow-bleed language and to recognize when short-form reputational risk is materializing. The plan is production-ready at Phases 1–6 with these four gaps addressed at design time (not retrofit during Phase 3 or 4).

## TOP 3 FINDINGS BY IMPACT

1. **MAJOR: Czech language silent degradation** (§2.7, §2.8, §13.2) — Per-run candidate-set size is not metered; operators must manually police language equity weekly. Add a per-language volume floor (config-set, alarmable) or lock Czech curation as a required ritual.

2. **MAJOR: Czech short-form revisit trigger is deferred governance** (OD-22, §15 R-23, §3.1) — The six design commitments enforce recipe and register but do not measure success. Lock OD-22 to a concrete trigger (volume or time) BEFORE Phase 4, with Phase 4 acceptance including 5+ test runs to build baseline.

3. **MAJOR: Mapping distance has no platform fit check** (§6.9, §14.1, S-4) — Far-distance topics (no offer) produce different outcomes on LinkedIn (good) vs. TikTok (suspended). Add platform-aware framing rules or an advisory that short-form defaults to adjacent-or-closer distance.
