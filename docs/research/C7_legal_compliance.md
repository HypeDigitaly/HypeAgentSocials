## 1. What this means for the operator (plain language)

Reddit is the one P0 research source with a real legal wall around it: Reddit has locked down bulk/automated access since 2024 and tightened it further with a 2025/2026 "Responsible Builder Policy" — any commercial use, including using Reddit content to *power* a paid marketing product (not just reselling Reddit data itself), needs Reddit's written approval and a paid commercial contract. There is no evidence of an accessible "buy compliant Reddit data from a licensed reseller" shortcut for a company our size — the known licensing deals (Google, OpenAI) are bilateral training-data deals, not open resale programs. The safe default for v1 is a human operator manually reading Reddit and writing down observations — no automated API pulls, no scraping. X/Twitter reads are already dropped for v1 by your own decision, so the only live X question is the *publish* side: posting scheduled drafts through Postiz under your own account's authorization is the normal, allowed pattern, as long as Postiz itself isn't running afoul of X's own developer terms (a vendor-diligence item, not a HypeDigitaly-direct risk).

LinkedIn, TikTok Creative Center, and Google (Trends/search results) should all go on a **do-not-scrape list** by default: LinkedIn's terms and robots.txt actively block automated collection, TikTok's terms do the same for Creative Center, and — importantly — the one official low-friction Google API that used to cover this (Custom Search JSON API) is now **closed to new customers** in 2026. Meta Ad Library is the one bright spot: an official API exists, but it requires a named human to upload a government ID to Meta and get identity-verified — plan for that as an onboarding step, not a technical detail.

The EU AI Act's Article 50 transparency rules became legally binding on **2 August 2026** — this week, relative to this brief — with no size exemption and fines up to €15M or 3% of global turnover. Every AI-generated video, image, or voice clip the pipeline produces needs a visible/audible "this is AI" disclosure baked into the rendered file itself, because embedded provenance metadata (C2PA) gets stripped the moment any of Instagram, X, LinkedIn, TikTok, or Facebook re-encodes the upload — so metadata alone will never satisfy this obligation. On top of the EU law, each platform has its own separate labeling button/policy (TikTok's AIGC label, YouTube's "altered or synthetic content" toggle, Meta's "AI Info" label) that must be set correctly at publish time or the content risks removal or reach suppression — these are platform contract risks, independent of the EU law.

Public Reddit posts are personal data under GDPR even though they're public — storing full raw threads indefinitely is not the safe default; extracts with a bounded retention window are. If the pipeline ever uses a synthetic AI presenter modeled on a real person's face or voice (an employee, a stock actor, anyone identifiable), Czech law requires that person's consent, independent of any platform rule. Finally, Kie.ai (or any model router) does not itself grant you commercial rights — the actual grant comes from whichever specific upstream model produced a given asset, and router terms of this type (confirmed via two directly comparable products, OpenRouter and Replicate) explicitly push that risk onto the customer. The architecture must record, per generated asset, exactly which model made it and a snapshot of that model's license at the time — without that record you cannot defend a rights challenge later.

### 2.1 Reddit — commercial-use ToS posture (F-1 verdict input)

Reddit's legal posture toward commercial actors tightened materially between May 2024 and 2026 across four stacked documents: the User Agreement, the Data API Terms, the (2024/2025) Public Content Policy, and the newest **Responsible Builder Policy**, plus a rewritten `robots.txt`.

- Reddit's own framing (as reported by TechCrunch/Axios, May 2024, still the operative framing into 2026): "you can use Reddit content for non-commercial uses, such as learning and community, but need to talk to Reddit if you have commercial purposes in mind." Any company wanting to use Reddit data to **"power, augment or enhance products for commercial purposes"** needs a contract and payment.
  → **Severity: blocker (for automated/API collection)** | **Architecture area:** List A research/extraction layer (Reddit adapter) | **Design consequence:** Using research findings to feed a paid marketing-content product is "powering a commercial product" under Reddit's own framing, even though the pipeline never republishes Reddit content verbatim. Any automated Reddit API/bulk-collection design must be gated behind an explicit "Reddit commercial contract obtained" flag that defaults to **off**.

- Reddit's **Responsible Builder Policy** (reported effective date varies across secondary sources — "late 2025" per one source, "March 2026" per another; direct fetch blocked): **"You must not sell, license, share, or otherwise commercialize Reddit data without express written approval,"** extending to **"commercial and non-commercial mining, scraping, or using data for purposes like ads targeting or to train machine learning or AI models."** It replaced self-service OAuth app registration with an approval-ticket process; free-tier access is described as **"approved, personal, non-commercial use"** with monetizing free-tier-built applications called a ToS violation.
  → **Severity: blocker (automated path)** | **Architecture area:** cron/scheduled extraction module, secrets/config policy | **Design consequence:** Do not design an unattended cron job that pulls Reddit via unofficial wrappers/API keys obtained under the free/personal tier — this is an explicit non-commercial-only tier and would be a direct contract breach the moment output feeds commercial content.

- Reddit's `robots.txt` was updated to **"disallow all web crawling"** for unrecognized agents; Reddit rate-limits or blocks bots without an agreement, and has litigated against Anthropic, Perplexity, and SerpApi over unauthorized scraping (2025–2026, widely reported).
  → **Severity: blocker** | **Architecture area:** browser-automation policy (Playwright) | **Design consequence:** Reddit must be placed on the do-not-scrape list for any Playwright-style browser automation, not just for API wrappers — the robots.txt posture and litigation pattern apply to browsing automation too, not only to API calls.

**The real options for a commercial actor, ranked:**

1. **Official commercial API contract** — requires filing a request through Reddit's approval form, choosing "commercial," and (if accepted) a paid agreement reported around **$0.24 per 1,000 API calls** plus whatever minimum commitment/vetting Reddit applies. Approval criteria and minimums are opaque from public sources.
   → **Severity: major (cost/uncertainty), not a blocker in principle** | **Architecture area:** vendor/legal ops, not pure architecture | **Design consequence:** Treat as a later-phase option gated on volume justifying the spend and a human securing the contract — do not assume this path is available by default.

2. **Licensed aggregators/resellers** — no evidence found of an open, accessible reseller program that a company of HypeDigitaly's size could simply buy into. The known public deals (Google ≈$60M, OpenAI) are bilateral training-data licenses for that specific licensee's own product, not a marketplace for third parties to purchase compliant Reddit datasets.
   → **Severity: major (this option is likely not real for us)** | **Architecture area:** List A source-policy registry | **Design consequence:** Do not architect around an assumed "buy a Reddit data feed from a compliant vendor" path without a named, verified vendor contract in hand that explicitly grants redistribution/analysis rights to a third party like HypeDigitaly.

3. **Human-curated manual reading** (recommended default for v1) — an operator reads Reddit in an ordinary browser session and manually writes summaries/observations into the theme's research notes. No automated means, no bulk storage of raw API responses, no redistribution. This sidesteps the *contract*-breach exposure (Reddit's restrictions target automated/bulk/commercializing use of its data feed, not a human reading a public page), but does **not** sidestep GDPR exposure once a human writes down a specific person's quote/complaint (see §2.6).
   → **Severity: n/a (this is the mitigation)** | **Architecture area:** List A research/extraction layer, operator workflow | **Design consequence:** Default v1 Reddit "extraction method" = human-curated manual review, explicitly excluded from the automation/cron scope; if automated Reddit collection is ever added later, it must route through option 1 first.

### 2.2 X/Twitter — publish-side + storage/deriving rules (reads out of scope for v1)

Per the operator's decision, X research reads are dropped for v1, so this section is deliberately a snapshot, not a full extraction-policy analysis.

- **Publish side:** X's own automation guidance (multiple 2026 secondary sources, converging) treats scheduling your own posts through an authorized third-party tool as explicitly fine: *"You can schedule your own tweets to publish at a future time using any authorized third-party tool, and this is completely fine."* Prohibited automation is engagement-side: mass follow/unfollow, bulk liking/retweeting, spam DMs, duplicate content across accounts, and — separately — scraping via browser automation.
  → **Severity: minor** | **Architecture area:** Postiz publish adapter | **Design consequence:** Posting HypeDigitaly's own approved drafts via Postiz's scheduling on the company's own authorized account is within X's allowed pattern. The open item is vendor diligence, not a HypeDigitaly-direct legal risk: confirm Postiz operates an official, paid-tier X API integration (not scraping) so that its own compliance failures don't become an availability risk for us.

- **X Developer Agreement** (effective **27 April 2026**, directly fetched): defines **"Commercial Use"** as use "by or for a business... or as part of a product or service that is monetized"; prohibits using the X API/content to **"fine-tune or train a foundation or frontier model,"** bars aggregating/caching location data except attached to its content, bars creating derivative works of licensed material without authorization, and requires deletion of content within a set window on takedown/deletion signals.
  → **Severity: n/a for v1 (deferred)** | **Architecture area:** future X-reads adapter (not built in v1) | **Design consequence:** If X reads are ever added later, the same non-caching / no-AI-training / delete-propagation obligations that apply to Reddit-style sources apply here too, and would need their own storage-retention design; flagged as an open decision, not built now.

### 2.3 LinkedIn, Meta Ad Library, TikTok Creative Center, Google Trends/SERP — DO-NOT-SCRAPE list

**LinkedIn (public posts/profiles/feed) — DO NOT SCRAPE, no automation.**
LinkedIn's User Agreement (effective **3 November 2025**, directly fetched), Section 8.2, explicitly prohibits: *"Develop, support or use software, devices, scripts, robots or any other means or processes (such as crawlers, browser plugins and add-ons or any other technology) to scrape or copy the Services"*; *"Use bots or other unauthorized automated methods to access the Services... create, comment on, like, share, or re-share posts, or otherwise drive inauthentic engagement"*; and *"Copy, use, display or distribute any information (including content) obtained from the Services... without the consent of the content owner."* LinkedIn's `robots.txt` (directly fetched) blocks essentially all non-LinkedIn-bot crawlers from profile, connections, messaging, job-search, network/people-discovery, groups, learning, analytics, and internal API paths (`/voyager/api`, `/oauth2/v2/*`), while allowing only LinkedIn's own bot.
→ **Severity: blocker** | **Architecture area:** List A extraction method registry, Playwright policy | **Design consequence:** LinkedIn public content goes on the do-not-scrape list outright — no Playwright, no unofficial API wrapper. Note that the well-known *hiQ v. LinkedIn* litigation narrowed a specific US criminal-statute (CFAA) claim in one fact pattern; it is **not** a general scraping safe harbor and does not neutralize LinkedIn's contract-enforcement or technical-blocking rights. Default extraction method: human-curated manual browsing only. LinkedIn's own Marketing/Partner APIs exist but are scoped to managing your *own* company page/ads, not researching third-party public content.

**Meta Ad Library — official API path exists; use it instead of scraping the public site.**
Access to the Ad Library API requires identity verification through Meta for Developers — uploading a government ID at `facebook.com/ID`, confirming country of residence, with a 1–3 business day review — plus acceptance of the Ad Library API terms as a condition of API access (converging secondary sources; direct fetch of `facebook.com/ads/library/api` was blocked in this session).
→ **Severity: major (process friction, not a legal blocker)** | **Architecture area:** List A extraction adapter (Meta Ad Library) | **Design consequence:** Build the Meta Ad Library adapter against the official Graph-API-based Ad Library API, not the public website UI. Budget an onboarding step where a named natural person at HypeDigitaly completes ID verification before this source can be automated at all; until that is done, treat Ad Library as human-curated-browse-only too.

**TikTok Creative Center — DO NOT bulk-export; browse only, or apply for the official Research/Commercial Content API.**
TikTok's Terms of Service (converging secondary sources) prohibit users from *"scraping, crawling, exporting or otherwise extracting any data or content in any form for any purpose from the Platform using any automated system or software, including automated 'bots,' except as approved in writing by TikTok,"* explicitly extending to Creative Center data, reinforced by an April 2026 rule update tying scraping to Commercial Content API ToS violations where that API applies. TikTok's robots.txt reportedly disallows broad crawling.
→ **Severity: blocker (automation)** | **Architecture area:** List A extraction method registry | **Design consequence:** Creative Center is a human-facing dashboard product — treat it as intended: an operator views trend/format dashboards interactively (this is the product's designed use) and records observations manually; do not build an automated exporter against it. If systematic bulk data is ever needed, the only sanctioned path is applying for TikTok's Research API or Commercial Content API (approval-gated, not available by default).

**Google Trends / Google Search results (SERP) — DO NOT SCRAPE; official low-friction API path has closed.**
Google's Terms of Service (effective **30 July 2026**, directly fetched) state users must not engage in *"using automated means to access content from any of our services in violation of the machine-readable instructions on our web pages (for example, robots.txt files that disallow crawling, training, or other activities)."* Google has litigated against SerpApi over SERP scraping in 2026. Critically, **Google's Custom Search JSON API — the previous official low-friction path for programmatic search-results access — is now closed to new customers** (directly fetched, `developers.google.com/custom-search/v1/overview`): existing customers only, sunset **1 January 2027**; new adopters are directed to Vertex AI Search or a sales conversation. A check of Google's current BigQuery public-dataset listing page did not show a Google Trends dataset among the samples listed (this is a negative/unconfirmed result, not a confirmed absence — recheck).
→ **Severity: major** | **Architecture area:** List A extraction method registry (Google Trends/SERP source) | **Design consequence:** No confirmed official, low-friction Google API path exists for HypeDigitaly as a new 2026 adopter. Default: human-curated interactive use of the public Trends UI (explicitly designed for public browsing — checking it is fine, bulk automated pulling is not) for demand-signal research; treat any third-party "SERP/Trends API" reseller (DataForSEO-style, SerpApi-style) as a vendor that has itself accepted Google's ToS risk on your behalf — that is a documented vendor-risk decision to make explicitly if pursued, not a default-safe shortcut.

**Consolidated DO-NOT-SCRAPE list (v1 default, automated/Playwright collection disabled):** Reddit (unless a commercial contract is signed), LinkedIn, TikTok Creative Center, Google Trends website, Google Search results pages, Meta Ad Library **website** (use the API instead), X/Twitter (already out of scope for v1 reads).
**Allowed-with-official-channel:** Meta Ad Library **API** (after ID verification).
**Allowed-as-human-only:** all of the above, for manual/interactive operator browsing and note-taking (no bulk retrieval, no automated storage of full page content).

### 2.4 EU AI Act Article 50 — synthetic/AI-generated marketing content transparency (in force)

Regulation (EU) 2024/1689, **Article 50**, entered into force for these transparency duties on **2 August 2026** (no size-based carve-out for this article; only a to-2-December-2026 grace period for existing generative systems' marking/detection tech already on the market). Penalties: **up to €15 million or 3% of worldwide annual turnover, whichever is higher**; scope is extraterritorial — it *"applies globally to providers, deployers, importers and distributors of AI systems that place AI on the EU market or whose AI outputs are used within the European Union"* (Cooley law-firm client alert, dated 2026-08-03, directly fetched) — so a Czech/EU company generating content for EU and global audiences is squarely in scope regardless of size.

Operative paragraphs (quoted/closely paraphrased from the official text via a specialist EU-AI-Act explainer site and the European Commission's own FAQ, both directly fetched, cross-checked against each other):

- **Art. 50(2)** — providers of AI systems generating synthetic audio, image, video, or text must ensure outputs are **"marked in a machine-readable format and detectable as artificially generated or manipulated,"** with technical solutions required to be *"effective, interoperable, robust and reliable as far as this is technically feasible."* Exemption for AI performing an *"assistive function for standard editing"* (e.g., a small edit/cleanup pass, not full generation).
- **Art. 50(4)** — deployers must disclose that content is **"artificially generated or manipulated"** for (a) deepfakes, and (b) AI-generated/manipulated text published to inform the public on matters of public interest, **"unless [it] has undergone a process of human review or editorial control and a natural or legal person holds editorial responsibility."**
- **Art. 50(5)** — disclosure must be given **"in a clear and distinguishable manner at the latest at the time of the first interaction or exposure,"** and must meet accessibility requirements.
- **Art. 50(1)/(3)** — separate obligations (chatbot/direct-interaction disclosure; emotion-recognition/biometric-categorization disclosure) are not the primary fit for this pipeline but are noted for completeness.
- **Art. 3(60)** — **'deep fake'** is defined as *"AI-generated or manipulated image, audio or video content that resembles existing persons, objects, places, entities or events and would falsely appear to a person to be authentic or truthful."*
- Carve-out: content that is *"evidently artistic, creative, satirical, fictional or analogous"* needs only disclosure *"in an appropriate manner that does not hamper the display or enjoyment"* of the work — unlikely to cleanly cover ordinary B2B/consumer marketing content, which is not typically "evidently" artistic/satirical.
- **AI Office Code of Practice on Transparency of AI-Generated Content** (voluntary but confers a presumption-of-conformity benefit for signatories): developing a **standardized EU "AI" label icon**, modality-specific labeling (persistent for video, visible for images, audible for audio), with a final version targeted around mid-2026 (status as of this review: still finalizing — recheck urgently, this is time-sensitive).

→ **Severity: blocker (compliance gate)** | **Architecture area:** media generation/rendering pipeline, review package, publish-prep layer | **Design consequence:** Every AI-generated video/image/audio asset needs a **burned-in, human-perceivable AI disclosure** (on-screen label for video/image, audible statement for voice) applied at render time — not left to metadata — because embedded provenance will not survive platform re-encoding (§2.4a / F-8 below). This is a hard technical gate: an asset without this marking cannot be flagged "ready to publish" in the review package, regardless of platform-native labels (§2.5), because Art. 50 is a separate, EU-law-level obligation with its own penalty regime.

→ **Severity: major (open legal question)** | **Architecture area:** human-approval/review-gate design | **Design consequence:** The Art. 50(4) "human review/editorial control + editorial responsibility" carve-out maps naturally onto this project's existing mandatory human-approval gate — but the secondary sources reviewed describe this carve-out primarily in connection with the **text/public-interest** limb of 50(4)(b); it is **not confirmed** from the sources gathered whether/how cleanly it extends to reduce the **deepfake** disclosure duty under 50(4)(a) for a synthetic on-screen presenter. **Do not assume the human-approval step alone removes the need for a visible AI/deepfake disclosure on synthetic presenter video** — treat this as an open question for qualified EU AI Act counsel, and default to labeling regardless of human review having occurred, since over-disclosure carries no penalty while under-disclosure does.

**2.4a — C2PA / provenance-metadata state (confirms F-8).** Convergent secondary sources (four independent write-ups, 2026) confirm: major platforms — Instagram, X, LinkedIn, TikTok, Facebook — **re-encode nearly every uploaded image and video, which strips C2PA Content Credentials in the process** ("effectively 100% removal" is the characterization used); for video specifically, C2PA is typically carried in container-level structures (e.g., MP4 JUMBF boxes) that do not survive remuxing/re-encoding either. Where a platform's own systems read the manifest before stripping it (e.g., to power their own "AI Info"-style label), the record is consumed server-side for the platform's benefit, not preserved in the file a viewer could later re-verify.
→ **Severity: blocker (for any design relying on metadata-only compliance)** | **Architecture area:** rendering pipeline output spec, provenance record store | **Design consequence:** C2PA/metadata embedding is worth doing (harmless, sometimes used by platforms for their own auto-labeling) but **must never be the sole compliance mechanism**. The visible/audible label burned into the rendered pixels/audio is the load-bearing control; the provenance record (model, timestamp, license snapshot — see §2.8) belongs in the run/review package, not just the file's metadata, since the file's metadata will not survive distribution.

### 2.5 Platform-native AI-label obligations (separate from the AI Act — contractual/reach risk, not EU-law penalty risk)

- **TikTok:** AIGC label required when content is *"fully or significantly created/altered by AI"* and contains *"realistic images, audio or video"* — disclosed via TikTok's built-in AI-content toggle, or a sticker/caption. TikTok's own framing: the label is *"a disclosure mechanism, not a distribution signal"* — i.e., correct disclosure should not by itself suppress reach (2026 convergent secondary sources; TikTok's own Newsroom post on the labeling feature is the anchor primary source).
  → **Severity: major** | **Architecture area:** Postiz/publish-prep TikTok adapter | **Design consequence:** Publish-prep must set TikTok's AI-content disclosure flag for any wholly/substantially AI-generated short-video asset before hand-off to Postiz.

- **YouTube:** the "altered or synthetic content" disclosure toggle in Creator Studio/upload flow is required when a viewer *"could easily mistake"* the content *"for a real person, place, scene, or event,"* with **"digital face replacement"** explicitly named as a trigger — directly relevant to any synthetic/avatar presenter. Not required for clearly unrealistic/animated content, or for generative AI used only for production assistance (scripts, captions). As of a reported May 2026 rollout, YouTube runs **automatic AI detection** and can apply the label itself and penalize consistent non-disclosure (removal, Partner Program suspension).
  → **Severity: major** | **Architecture area:** publish-prep YouTube Shorts adapter | **Design consequence:** Set this attribute per video at publish-prep; do not rely on silence — YouTube's own detection can catch un-disclosed synthetic content after the fact, converting a labeling omission into an enforcement event.

- **Meta (Facebook/Instagram):** the **"AI Info"** label mechanism (Meta's own newsroom post, 5 April 2024, updated through 23 October 2025, directly fetched) applies on detection of industry-standard AI indicators or self-disclosure. Convergent 2026 secondary sources describe this maturing into an **enforced advertising requirement**: Ads Manager has a disclosure control advertisers "must" use for AI-generated visuals/text/audio in paid creative, and content judged **deceptive** (realistic depictions of events that did not occur) can receive a more prominent warning label and substantially reduced distribution (reported up to ~80% reach reduction for deceptive AI voice/music in video).
  → **Severity: major** | **Architecture area:** later-phase Meta Ads creative path (paid creative is explicitly a later-phase item in the assignment) | **Design consequence:** Because automatic detection depends on indicators that re-encoding can destroy (§2.4a), **self-disclosure at the Ads Manager/API level is the only reliable route** — the architecture cannot depend on Meta's own detection to satisfy this.

- **LinkedIn:** no single confirmed structured "AI label" toggle equivalent to the above three was found in the sources gathered. LinkedIn's 2026 policy update ("Keeping conversations real on LinkedIn," per convergent secondary sources) tightens authenticity expectations: disclosure is expected **"when AI materially generated or transformed content,"** with **per-post** transparency expected (a generic, profile-level AI disclaimer is explicitly called insufficient) — feeding organic-ranking and abuse-report signals.
  → **Severity: minor-to-major (monitor)** | **Architecture area:** publish-prep LinkedIn adapter, caption/copy template | **Design consequence:** Until a confirmed structured toggle exists, attach a short per-post AI-disclosure line in the LinkedIn copy itself for any substantially AI-generated visual asset; revisit if LinkedIn ships a dedicated control.

### 2.6 GDPR on collected research artifacts (feeds brief B2 raw-artifact storage design)

- **Public Reddit posts/comments (and equivalent public social content) ARE personal data.** A persistent username plus post/comment history is generally sufficient to make a natural person identifiable (directly or by combination); per GDPR's own logic on pseudonymization (Recital 26), pseudonymous data remains personal data — only true anonymization escapes the Regulation. This is the governing premise for everything else in this section.
  → **Severity: major** | **Architecture area:** raw research artifact store | **Design consequence:** Treat every stored Reddit thread/comment as personal data from the moment of storage, not just "web content."

- **Lawful basis:** consent from thousands of anonymous Redditors is not realistic; **legitimate interest (Art. 6(1)(f))** is the applicable basis. The EDPB's **"Guidelines on web scraping in the context of generative AI"** (reported approved 7 July 2026, per convergent secondary sources; exact guideline number not independently confirmed — recheck) requires a documented, **case-by-case, three-part legitimate-interest assessment** (purpose/interest test, necessity test, balancing test) — *"there are no blanket legitimate interest assessments for this purpose."* The EDPB separately flags that scraped social content can **residually capture special-category data** (political opinion, health, sexual orientation implied by community/subreddit context) and expects pre- and post-collection filtering to exclude it, plus a **published privacy notice** describing the activity even where individual per-person notice is impractical (Art. 14(5)(b) disproportionate-effort exception narrows, but does not eliminate, the general transparency duty).
  → **Severity: blocker (without a documented LIA)** | **Architecture area:** research/collection module, operator-facing compliance artifact | **Design consequence:** Before any Reddit-derived data (even human-curated notes quoting a specific person) is stored for reuse, HypeDigitaly needs a written legitimate-interest assessment for this specific purpose, and a published privacy notice covering the activity — these are company/legal artifacts the architecture should assume exist and reference, not something the software itself can generate.

- **Czech DPA (ÚOOÚ) practice** (directly fetched, `uoou.gov.cz`, undated page but current guidance): **"veřejnost údajů neznamená možnost jejich dalšího bezmezného zpracování"** (public availability of data does not mean unlimited further processing is permitted) — the same GDPR principles apply to internet-sourced personal data as to any other processing; the legitimate-interest balancing test is the operative safeguard, and data subjects retain an Art. 21 right to object, which the controller must honor absent compelling overriding grounds.
  → **Severity: major** | **Architecture area:** raw research artifact store, dedupe/identity handling | **Design consequence:** Design must be able to locate and delete a specific person's stored content on objection/erasure request — this argues against monolithic, hard-to-query raw dumps and for structured, queryable extract records.

- **Retention and minimization — reasoned recommendation, not a quoted legal minimum:** GDPR's storage-limitation principle (Art. 5(1)(e)) requires retention "no longer than necessary for the purpose," without specifying a day-count for this use case. Given the purpose here (scoring a topic candidate's freshness/virality for a marketing angle) has a naturally short shelf life, the reasoned recommendation is: **default to a short, bounded retention window for verbatim raw text (e.g., in the tens of days, a specific number is an open design decision) and keep de-identified derived signals (topic label, keyword, aggregate virality/sentiment score, source URL, timestamp) indefinitely instead of the original text/username.**
  → **Severity: minor (design guidance, not a hard legal number)** | **Architecture area:** raw research artifact store (feeds B2) | **Design consequence:** Store extracts-first; store full raw verbatim thread text only when needed for human audit of authenticity, on the same short retention clock; hash or drop the Reddit username from long-term storage unless it is specifically needed for dedup logic, in which case hash it rather than keep it in clear text.

### 2.7 Likeness/voice consent for avatar/UGC-style synthetic presenters

- **Czech Civil Code (89/2012 Sb.)** personality-rights framework (Czech Wikipedia synthesis of the statute; direct statutory-text fetch was blocked — recheck against primary text before build sign-off): use of a person's **likeness (podobizna)/image** and **voice recordings** generally requires that person's **consent (svolení)**. Statutory no-consent exceptions exist only for **official, scientific, artistic, or news-reporting** purposes, and only where the use does not conflict with the person's legitimate interests. Remedies for unauthorized use include cessation of the intrusion, removal of consequences, and compensation (moral or financial).
  → **Severity: blocker (if a real identifiable person is depicted)** | **Architecture area:** media provider adapter (avatar/voice tools), review-package gate | **Design consequence:** Ordinary commercial marketing content is **not** one of the statutory exceptions (it is neither news, science, art, nor an official act) — so any synthetic presenter/avatar that is modeled on or resembles a specific real identifiable person (an employee used as a "founder avatar," a licensed stock actor, any real public figure used in a testimonial-style clip) requires documented, scope-limited consent (specific use, duration, platforms, revocability) **independent of AI Act or platform rules**.

- **Wholly synthetic, non-resembling personas:** if the avatar/voice is entirely fictional and does not resemble any specific identifiable real person, Czech personality-rights consent obligations do not attach (no identifiable natural person is depicted) — but the AI Act Art. 50(2) marking duty and the platform-native labels in §2.5 still apply, because those trigger on "realistic" synthetic media generally, not on resemblance to a specific real individual.
  → **Severity: n/a (distinguishing case)** | **Architecture area:** media provider adapter metadata schema | **Design consequence:** The system should record, per avatar/voice asset, an explicit flag: "wholly synthetic, no real-person basis" vs. "modeled on a real identifiable person" — this flag determines whether the consent-record gate applies.

- **Employee-likeness special case:** if a real HypeDigitaly employee's face/voice is used, this is both a personality-rights matter (civil code) and likely an **employment-law** matter, since ordinary employment/contractor agreements typically do not include a perpetual marketing-likeness license by default.
  → **Severity: major** | **Architecture area:** HR/legal action item, not pure software architecture | **Design consequence:** Flag for a separate signed release/addendum before any employee-likeness avatar is used; the review-package gate should require a reference to this release, same as for any third-party likeness.

- **Platform layer stacks on top, not instead of:** even with valid consent, TikTok/YouTube/Meta's own synthetic-media disclosure policies (§2.5) still require labeling — consent solves the personality-rights exposure, not the platform-disclosure duty; both apply cumulatively.

### 2.8 Generated-asset commercial-rights layering (router vs. upstream model — "weakest link" analysis)

Kie.ai's own Terms of Service could not be directly retrieved in this session (blocked on every path attempted, no archived snapshot found) — this is itself flagged as a recheck item. In its place, this section analyzes the **legal structure** using two directly comparable, currently-live "model router" products whose terms were successfully fetched in full: **OpenRouter** (effective 29 July 2026) and **Replicate** (effective 1 April 2026). Both are structurally the same category of product as Kie.ai — a service that fronts multiple third-party upstream generative models behind one API/account — so the pattern below should be treated as **strong structural evidence of how this class of product is legally built**, not as a literal quote from Kie.ai. Specific upstream model names (e.g., any Veo-class, Kling-class, or Runway-class video model) are **ASSUMED INPUT (from A2)** — this brief does not assert which models Kie.ai actually routes to.

- **OpenRouter:** *"Your ownership rights in the Output are set forth in the Model Terms for each Model you use"*; *"You are solely responsible for reviewing the Model Terms applicable to each Model before accessing or using that Model"*; *"OpenRouter does not modify, waive, or limit any Model Terms unless expressly stated in writing"*; disclaims warranty on *"any Model Provider's data handling, retention, training, security, availability, or intellectual property practices"*; disclaims liability for suspension/removal/modification of any model arising from the model provider's own acts; Model Providers hold **third-party-beneficiary** rights to enforce the agreement against the customer.
- **Replicate:** grants customer ownership of outputs **"subject to any Third Party Terms (as determined by the Models you use to generate the Output)"**; *"You are solely responsible for complying with all Third Party Terms and Replicate is not... a party to any separate Third Party Terms"*; disclaims liability for Marketplace Models' *"security, functionality, operation, availability"* and for whether third-party model offerings comply with copyright or other law.

→ **Severity: blocker (for defensibility of any "approved" commercial asset)** | **Architecture area:** media provider adapter, run/review-package schema | **Design consequence:** A router-class provider's own brand terms will not, by themselves, grant blanket commercial-use rights — the **weakest link is always the specific upstream model** that generated a given asset, and its commercial-use terms can differ by model and can change over time independent of the router. The architecture must record, **per generated asset** (not once per provider, not once per system): (1) the exact routed model identity and version; (2) generation timestamp; (3) a **snapshot/citation of that model's applicable commercial-use terms as they existed at generation time** (a live link is not sufficient evidence later, since terms drift); (4) the router's own transaction/order ID as a secondary evidence trail. An asset missing this record should fail the "ready to publish" gate — treat as a hard block, not a warning, because router-class terms of this type make the **model provider a third-party beneficiary who can enforce against the customer directly**, while the router disclaims its own liability for the provider's actions — meaning HypeDigitaly, not the router, is the party left holding the exposure if an upstream output infringes someone's IP or personality rights.

### 2.9 Czech-law specifics (advertising, consumer protection, ÚOOÚ/ČOI)

- **Advertising must be recognizable as advertising.** Czech law (Act No. 40/1995 Coll., on the Regulation of Advertising) is understood to require that advertising be clearly identifiable and not disguised as other content — this is standard, well-established Czech advertising law; the primary statutory text could not be directly retrieved in this session (`zakonyprolidi.cz` blocked), so this citation is flagged **low-medium confidence / recheck** rather than a verbatim quote.
  → **Severity: major (recheck primary text)** | **Architecture area:** claim-safety/voice guardrail layer | **Design consequence:** Any content that reads as editorial/organic but is actually a paid promotion needs to be recognizably an ad; this is a legal requirement, not merely a platform-disclosure nicety, and should be checked alongside the platform-native sponsored-content labels.

- **Misleading/unverifiable marketing claims are independently illegal, not just off-brand.** Czech consumer-protection law (understood to be Act No. 634/1992 Coll., on Consumer Protection, implementing the EU Unfair Commercial Practices Directive 2005/29/EC) prohibits misleading commercial practices, including false or unverifiable factual claims. This gives **independent legal force** to the assignment's own "never invent prices, ROI, client names, or fake proof" rule — it is not only a brand-voice preference, it is a regulatory compliance control.
  → **Severity: major** | **Architecture area:** claim-safety/voice guardrail layer, review package | **Design consequence:** The claim-safety check should be logged per generated asset (what was checked, what was blocked/rewritten) as an evidentiary trail — this doubles as regulatory-defensibility documentation, not just a QA log.

- **Czech Trade Inspection Authority (ČOI)** (directly fetched, `coi.gov.cz/en`) is the relevant market-supervision authority for consumer/advertising compliance generally (e-shop inspections, discount-compliance checks, product safety); the specific page reviewed did not itself detail advertising-claim enforcement mechanics, so treat this as a low-specificity confirmation of the correct authority, not a full enforcement-procedure citation.
- **Czech GDPR regulator** is Úřad pro ochranu osobních údajů (ÚOOÚ) — the correct national reference point alongside EDPB guidance for §2.6.
